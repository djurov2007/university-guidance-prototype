# Studyguide — clearer programme choices with traceable conditions

**Local prototype • English-only • fictional examples • 11 October 2026**

## Problem and audience

Prospective bachelor, master's and PhD students compare scattered programme, budget, language and qualification requirements. Our hypothesis is that a structured conversation with visible evidence can clarify their next verification step. The first intended audience is invited adult candidates exploring a limited catalogue; global discovery is a direction, not current coverage.

## How it works

The student reviews a structured profile, confirms or corrects it and resolves conflicts. Deterministic rules check hard constraints and institutional conditions before ranking up to three options. Each option shows reasons, uncertainty, evidence identifiers, a check date and a next step. An alternative requires specific permission when it departs from a user constraint; permission cannot waive an institutional rule.

## What distinguishes the approach

Sources, self-reported qualifications and unknown conditions stay separate. Empty results and conditional options are visible. Possible scholarships are not treated as awarded money; no admission probability is promised. The model layer uses validated identifiers and structured outputs and cannot remove hard rules.

## What exists today

A local Next.js app, profile confirmation/conflicts, deterministic matching, fictional source details, simulated explanations, bounded invitations and server quotas. Seven synthetic profiles cover bachelor, master prerequisites, funded PhD, no-match, permission, conflict and recovery. Software/browser results are documented. Eight real partial staging records exist; **zero programmes are published or human-review approved**.

## What remains unproven

Real-model conversation quality, usefulness with students, complete/current institutional data, cloud operation and production/privacy readiness. Real uploads and paid AI are disabled. The dated QA audit retains five high development-tool findings. No paying users, traction or partnership with MBZUAI or another institution is claimed.

## What a future approved pilot should teach us

Can candidates complete a useful task, identify evidence/uncertainty and explain a concrete next action? Which questions confuse them, and what effort is needed to review programme facts? Testers are reviewers, not paying customers. Personal-data testing requires a separate approval and accountable operator.
